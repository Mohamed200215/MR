Registering a fishing vessel shouldn't require decoding legal text first

Redesigning the Merkeregister registration flow — the Norwegian Directorate of Fisheries' form for registering, updating and deregistering commercial fishing vessels.

01 — The problem

An occasional, high-stakes form written for lawyers, filled in by fishermen

Merkeregisteret is Fiskeridirektoratet's official registry of commercial fishing vessels. Owners must register a vessel (innmelding), report changes (endring) or deregister it (utmelding) through a generic government form on Altinn.

The people filling it in are vessel owners or their representatives. They do this occasionally — when buying a boat, changing ownership, selling — not routinely. Many are not digitally fluent, and none of them have the form's mental model memorised between uses.

The form itself works. Two things about it don't.

F1

Attachment requirements hidden in legal prose

Some situations require documentation — for example an updated shareholder register (aksjeeierbok), but only in certain ownership-change scenarios. Today this rule lives in a dense legal paragraph. Users discover what they needed after starting, or after a rejected submission.

F2

Corrections silently overwrite the record

To correct a submission you duplicate the old one and resubmit. The new submission replaces the old — entirely. Miss a field while re-entering, and correct data is lost without warning. The system offers no comparison, no confirmation of what changed.

02 — Design principles

Three commitments

Principle 1

Plain language, first time right

Requirements are stated as instructions, not statute. "You will need an updated shareholder register" — said before the form starts, not cited in §-text halfway through.

Principle 2

Only ask what's relevant

Innmelding, endring and utmelding need different information. A short situation step up front routes each user to only the questions and attachments their case requires.

Principle 3

Nothing lost by omission

Corrections start from the submitted record, pre-filled. Before submitting, every change is shown old-versus-new. Data can only leave the register deliberately.

03 — Key flow 1

New vessel registration, as a guided conditional flow

Instead of one generic form, registration opens with two or three plain questions about the user's situation. The answers determine which documents are required — and the user sees that complete checklist before the form begins. Screens below are from the clickable Figma prototype.

Screen A — Situation

A short pre-form step in everyday Norwegian. Each option carries a one-line description so users don't need to already know what innmelding means legally. The ownership question is what the current form hides in paragraph text — here it's just a question.

Screen B — Upfront checklist

The complete, personalised documentation list — before a single form field. Conditional items like the shareholder register appear only when relevant, highlighted, with the reason stated in one sentence ("because the vessel has multiple shareholders"), replacing the legal paragraph.

Users who don't have everything can save the checklist and return, instead of abandoning a half-filled form.

Screen C — The form itself

Short steps, visible progress, every field labelled in plain Norwegian with units and examples where they help. Only the questions this situation requires appear — a single-owner utmelding would see roughly half of this flow. Autosave is stated explicitly, because occasional users assume the opposite.

04 — Key flow 2

Correcting a registration without risking it

Today, corrections mean duplicating a past submission and re-entering data — and the resubmission silently replaces the record. The redesign treats a correction as an edit of the existing record, with an explicit review of every change before anything is overwritten.

Screen D — Pre-filled, not blank

Editing opens the submitted record, not an empty duplicate. Every field shows the register's current value, so there is nothing to re-enter and nothing to forget. Fields the user touches are marked immediately, with the previous value shown inline — the first line of defence against accidental changes.

Screen E — Change summary

Before final submission, a diff: old value against new value for every changed field, and an explicit statement that everything else is kept. Nothing can be lost by omission — an unchanged field simply stays. The submit button confirms changes, not a wholesale replacement.

05 — Accessibility

WCAG-driven decisions

Contrast beyond the minimum

Body text and form labels hold at least 7:1 against the background (WCAG 1.4.6 AAA), not just the 4.5:1 AA floor — this form is often filled in on deck, outdoors, on ageing screens.

Plain-language errors, next to the field

Errors say what to do ("Skriv lengden i meter, f.eks. 14,95"), appear beside the field they concern, and are announced to screen readers (WCAG 3.3.1, 3.3.3).

Real labels, always visible

Every input has a programmatically associated, permanently visible label — no placeholder-as-label (WCAG 1.3.1, 3.3.2). Units and examples sit in the label, not inside the field.

Logical focus order

Focus follows reading order through each step; conditional fields are inserted directly after the question that revealed them, and a visible focus ring is never removed (WCAG 2.4.3, 2.4.7).

06 — Reflection

What I'd validate next

This is a concept project, so its biggest assumptions are untested. With real vessel owners I'd want to verify that the situation questions use words they'd use themselves — "eierskifte" may be obvious to a caseworker and opaque to a first-time owner — and that the upfront checklist reduces abandoned submissions rather than just moving the drop-off earlier. With Fiskeridirektoratet's caseworkers I'd want to check whether the change-summary format matches how they actually review corrections, and whether pre-filling from the register is feasible given how Altinn submissions map to their case system today.
